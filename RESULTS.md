# Results: does hyperscaler capex growth predict cloud revenue growth?

Research question: does hyperscaler capex growth (lag 1-4 quarters) predict
cloud segment revenue YoY growth, and does the relationship change in the
2023+ regime? Target: `cloud_yoy` for MSFT (Intelligent Cloud), GOOGL (Google
Cloud), AMZN (AWS). All figures below come from `model.py` run directly
against the final, independently-verified dataset (see `VALIDATION_REPORT.md`
for data sourcing and an important disclosure about agent reliability during
data collection — every number here was regenerated by re-running the
pipeline, not taken from any agent's self-report).

## Sample sizes
Complete-case quarters (target + all 9 lagged features non-null):

| ticker | n |
|---|---|
| AMZN | 37 |
| GOOGL | 22 |
| MSFT | 39 |

GOOGL clears the project's minimum (8) but is the thinnest sample, and its
pre-2023 regime-split training set is only 8 quarters — read GOOGL's
regime-comparison numbers with that caveat.

## Stationarity (ADF) and cross-correlation (CCF)
`cloud_yoy` is **not clearly stationary** for any ticker at the 5% level
(AMZN p=0.061 borderline, GOOGL p=0.999 clearly non-stationary, MSFT p=0.55
non-stationary) — consistent with each company's cloud business having gone
through a multi-year deceleration trend (very high growth early, slowing as
the business matures) rather than fluctuating around a fixed mean. This is a
real limitation for the AR(1)/ARIMA component, which assumes stationarity;
results should be read as descriptive, not as evidence the series is
well-behaved for classical time-series models.

Most capex-growth features ARE stationary (ADF p<0.05 for `capex_yoy_lag1`
and `debt_yoy_lag1` in all three tickers; `fed_funds_chg_yoy_lag` stationary
for AMZN/MSFT). `capex_to_rev_lag1` and `fcf_margin_lag1` are not stationary
for any ticker (trending ratios).

Cross-correlation of `cloud_yoy` against `capex_yoy_lag1` at lags 0-4:
- **AMZN**: uniformly weak (0.07-0.11 across all lags) — no support for a
  leading-indicator relationship in this data.
- **GOOGL**: strongest at lag 0-1 (0.38, 0.38), decaying by lag 3-4 (0.13-0.17)
  — the correlation that exists looks more contemporaneous than leading.
- **MSFT**: weak at short lags, rising to lag 3-4 (0.21, 0.25) — the only
  ticker showing a pattern consistent with a longer-lag leading relationship,
  though still modest.

**Honest finding: the naive baseline (last quarter's cloud growth) wins or
ties for AMZN and GOOGL, and is only marginally beaten by XGBoost for MSFT
(0.052 vs 0.055 RMSE — a 5% improvement on n=27, not a strong result).**
Ridge/Lasso/XGBoost are often considerably *worse* than naive, especially for
AMZN. None of the engineered capex/macro features add real one-step-ahead
predictive power beyond the autoregressive term at this sample size.

## Regime test (H3): train ≤2022-12-31, test 2023+
A single Lasso fit per ticker on pre-2023 data, evaluated on 2023+ (not
walk-forward re-fit — this specifically tests whether a model frozen before
2023 still works after):

| ticker | n_train | n_test | Lasso RMSE | Lasso MAE | naive RMSE | naive MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AMZN | 23 | 14 | 0.246 | 0.237 | 0.035 | 0.026 |
| GOOGL | 8 | 14 | 0.215 | 0.155 | 0.082 | 0.059 |
| MSFT | 25 | 14 | 0.163 | 0.147 | 0.074 | 0.041 |

A pre-2023-trained Lasso generalizes **much worse** than the naive baseline
to the 2023+ period for all three tickers (3-7x worse RMSE) — consistent with
H3's premise that *something* about the relationship changed, but the nature
of that change (see next section) is not "capex became a stronger predictor."
It looks more like a level/volatility shift in cloud growth itself (the 2023+
AI infrastructure buildout era) that a model fit on the calmer pre-2023 period
simply didn't anticipate — not a strengthening capex→cloud leading relationship.

## Pre- vs post-2023 Lasso coefficients (H3)
Standardized coefficients, same 9 features, fit separately on each period:

| feature | pre-2023 (n=56) | post-2023 (n=42) |
|---|---|---|
| capex_yoy_lag1 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| capex_yoy_lag2 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| capex_yoy_lag3 | -0.000 | 0.000 |
| capex_yoy_lag4 | 0.005 | 0.000 |
| fed_funds_chg_yoy_lag | -0.008 | -0.021 |
| capex_to_rev_lag1 | -0.019 | 0.000 |
| fcf_margin_lag1 | -0.014 | 0.000 |
| debt_yoy_lag1 | 0.009 | 0.037 |
| cloud_yoy_lag1 (AR term) | 0.124 | 0.096 |

**Capex-growth lags are regularized to essentially zero in both periods** —
Lasso does not select them as useful predictors either before or after 2023.
If anything, the already-weak pre-2023 capex signal (lag4 only, coefficient
0.005) disappears entirely post-2023, the opposite of H1's hypothesis that
capex growth leads cloud growth. The autoregressive term (last quarter's
cloud growth) remains the dominant driver in both periods, though its weight
drops somewhat post-2023 (0.124 → 0.096), while the macro (fed funds) and
leverage (debt growth) terms gain weight — consistent with a 2023+ period
where cloud growth is less driven by its own recent momentum and more mixed
in with macro/financing conditions, but this is a secondary, weaker signal.

**Bottom line on the three hypotheses:**
- **H1 (capex growth lag 1-4 predicts cloud revenue growth)**: not supported.
  Lasso regularizes capex lags to ~zero in-sample; CCF shows only weak
  (AMZN), contemporaneous-looking (GOOGL), or longer-lag-but-modest (MSFT)
  correlations; no model beats the naive baseline by a meaningful margin.
- **H2 (implicit — do these engineered features improve forecasts at all)**:
  not supported one-step-ahead; naive wins or ties everywhere except a small
  MSFT edge for XGBoost.
- **H3 (relationship changes in 2023+ regime)**: partially supported in the
  sense that *forecasting performance* degrades sharply after 2023 for a
  model frozen pre-2023, but **not** in the sense of capex becoming a
  *stronger* predictor — it becomes, if anything, a slightly weaker one
  (coefficient drops to exactly zero). The regime change looks like a shift
  in the cloud-growth process itself (level and/or volatility), not a
  strengthening capex→cloud transmission channel.

## Limitations (read before trusting any of the above)
- Small samples (14-39 complete-case quarters per ticker); RMSE/MAE and
  Lasso coefficients at this n have wide uncertainty — none of this should be
  treated as a precise estimate, only as a directional finding.
- GOOGL's segment history only goes back to Q4 2019 (first true YoY base),
  making its pre-2023 training set (n=8) the least reliable regime comparison.
- MSFT's FY2024 quarters (4 of them) are excluded from the main walk-forward
  table because of the segment recast described in `VALIDATION_REPORT.md`
  section 6 — a real structural break, not a modeling artifact, and it sits
  exactly on the 2023+ regime boundary being studied.
- Capex and cloud revenue are **not AI-specific** measures — both include
  non-AI infrastructure and non-cloud business lines respectively (per the
  project's own design constraint). Any apparent relationship (or absence of
  one) is about aggregate hyperscaler capex vs. aggregate cloud segment
  revenue, not an AI-investment-specific claim.
- `capex_total` understates real infrastructure spend for pre-2019 quarters
  (finance-lease data unavailable before ASC 842 adoption).
- MAPE is not reported: cloud_yoy values near zero (a few quarters) make
  MAPE numerically unstable/explosive; RMSE/MAE are the reported metrics per
  the project's own design decision.

## 2030 scenarios (bear/base/bull) — explicit assumptions, NOT a forecast
Given the above (no model beat naive, and the regime-split test shows the
relationship is unstable), the 2030 figures in `data/processed/scenario_2030.csv`
are **not** model-validated projections. They are a simple compounding
exercise from the last observed quarterly cloud revenue, using explicitly
assumed annual growth rates — bear 5%, base 15%, bull 30% — with an
illustrative, arbitrarily widening ±5%-per-year uncertainty band. They should
be presented as a labeled scenario/assumption exercise only, consistent with
the project's own design decision, never as "predicted 2030 revenue."
